Method & data

Precip type uses the Revised Bourgouin algorithm (Birk et al. 2021, Wea. Forecasting) on each member's wet-bulb profile; its four probabilities (rain, snow, freezing rain, sleet) split that member's QPF. Freezing rain is limited to a 2 m temperature of +1 °C or colder (sleet +3 °C), the warmer cases becoming rain. Where the model itself says at least 90 % of the precipitation is frozen, the freezing-rain share is also capped by its liquid fraction (100 − percent frozen precipitation), and for accumulations the shares are averaged over each 3-hr step rather than taken at its end.

NCEP dominant type is the type GEFS itself assigns each member (four wet-bulb schemes, ties resolved freezing rain > snow > sleet > rain). It is offered as an alternate map and as probabilities, and Agreement shows, among members where both methods name a type, how often they name the same one; low values mark places where the type depends on the method.

Snowfall = 3-hr QPF × snow share × snow-to-liquid ratio, summed over the period. The default is Kuchera (warmest temperature surface–500 mb, 12:1 baseline); the selector also offers a fixed 13:1, 12:1 or 10:1 applied to the snow liquid equivalent. Fixed ratios in the 12–13:1 range often verify better than 10:1 in the Northeast.

Freezing rain and sleet are the liquid-equivalent part of each member's QPF that the Bourgouin probabilities assign to that type. Freezing rain is not radial ice accretion (no wind, surface or icing-efficiency adjustment); sleet is liquid, not pellet depth.

Statistics are computed per grid cell from the 31 member totals (percentiles interpolate between ranked members). PMM (Ebert 2001) keeps the ensemble-mean pattern but takes its amplitudes from all members pooled over the map; LPMM (Clark 2017; Snook et al.) does the same inside a neighborhood around each point.

Periods are rolling windows ending at the selected hour. Before the window fits in the run, the total since initialization is shown.

Hover (desktop) or tap (phone) the map for the ensemble breakdown at that point. Click to pin.

Data: NOAA/NCEP GEFS 0.5°. Basemap: Natural Earth, US Census cartographic boundaries.
